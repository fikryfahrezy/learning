# Unsupervised Learning

How does an AI system learn through unsupervised learning? It begins with unlabeled data, such as these images of dogs. In unsupervised learning, unlike supervised learning, you don’t tell the AI what each image is. Instead, you let AI explore the images and identify structure on its own. For instance, how many types of dogs are there, based on these unlabeled images?

By measuring similarities and differences between all the images, the AI begins to group them into clusters without any human-provided labels. First, the AI learns to group the dogs by ear shape. It finds three clusters: prick ears, bat ears, and V-shaped ears.

Next, the system regroups the images by fur color, again without any labels. The images fall into three color groups: white, brown, and black.

Finally, by integrating ear shape, fur color, and other subtle visual cues, the AI created five finer clusters that align with breed distinctions. Through unsupervised learning, the AI began with raw, unlabeled images and perceived its own way to organize them. First by ear shape, then by fur color, then by breed. These steps illustrate how unsupervised learning enables AI to discover patterns and organize data into meaningful clusters without any labeled examples provided by humans.
