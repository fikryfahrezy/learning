# Did AI just make that up?

Have you ever trusted an answer or information given by an AI, only to learn the source never existed? Consider Priya, a content analyst, who asks an AI assistant for a supporting citation when she's writing a report. The AI provides Priya with “Doctor Emily Carter's 2020 study on market trends”, including a detailed summary. Priya includes the citation in her report until she learns that neither the researcher nor the paper is real. Priya is the recipient of an AI hallucination.

An AI hallucination is a fabricated or incorrect output that an AI model presents as if it were true. Hallucinations occur when the system generates content that has no basis in reality, fact, or data.

AI can provide convincing answers that appear correct, but they are merely fabricated and offered as true. AI can invent and present information as facts, people, or events that never existed.

Hallucinations occur because AI doesn’t “understand” the world. Instead, it predicts the next words based on patterns observed from its training data. The AI is not thinking or checking facts; it is only generating text that sounds plausible.

For example, when Priya types, “List top researchers in market analytics,” the AI returns a list of names, such as “Doctor Emily Carter, Stanford University” and provides detailed study summaries. After researching some of the names, Priya discovers neither the authors nor the papers exist.

Because the AI model prioritizes pattern matching over factual accuracy, it invented details when it couldn’t retrieve an honest answer.

Beyond fake citations, AI hallucinations can fabricate anything, from suggesting non-existent product features to creating false news stories.

Priya asks the AI tool for recent industry headlines. The AI replies, “Acme Corp. acquired a competitor last month”, quoting a non-existent chief executive officer. Priya begins her planning strategy around that acquisition, only to learn that the merger never happened.

So, what are the risks? AI hallucinations and errors can mislead someone into making decisions based on false information.

Citing non-existent studies might undermine a researcher’s credibility.

Marketing campaign leaders might allocate budgets based on imagined events.

Suggestions to include impossible features in product roadmaps might waste valuable time and resources.

To mitigate such risks and adverse outcomes, Priya can verify sources by cross-checking citations, quotes, or statistics against reliable databases and trusted search engines. She can verify plausibility by checking whether details align with known facts, such as whether a study or company exists. She can also use known tools and compare outputs from different AI models.

By understanding how and why AI hallucinations occur, Priya and others using AI tools can take simple steps to protect their work and avoid errors.

Whether you're writing a report, planning a campaign, or gathering research, don't assume AI answers or information is real. Ask yourself these questions:

- Does this make sense?
- Can I verify it?

AI can be powerful when used carefully. Stay curious, skeptical, and cross-check all AI information.

When used carefully, AI can enhance your research and creativity, just be mindful of the potential for hallucinations.
