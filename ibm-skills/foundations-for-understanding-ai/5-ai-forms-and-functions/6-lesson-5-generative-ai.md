## What is generative AI?

Every second, generative AI tools produce content from product ads and movie scripts to custom workout plans and software code. Generative AI turns patterns learned from large datasets into original outputs and drafts text, composes images and audio, or writes code, all based on prompts. Instead of copying, generative AI synthesizes what it has learned to produce something new that resembles the style and structure of its examples.

> **Generative AI** refers to systems that use deep learning models and their datasets to create original content, such as text, images, video, audio, or software code, in response to a user’s prompt or request. [1]

- [1] [What is generative AI?](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/generative-ai) (2024) IBM Think (Article) March 22, 2024

In practice, generative AI accelerates first drafts, sparks ideas, and adapts tone or format for different audiences, producing original outputs rather than reproducing copies. Its outputs are probabilistic and might include errors or gaps, so human review, fact-checking, and clear labeling are important. Generative AI complements other forms of AI by creating content that can then be analyzed, predicted upon, or used to inform decisions.

Generative AI systems create new content, such as text, images, or music, by learning from large sets of existing data. Generative AI tools produce original material that is similar to what they have learned, without being exact copies.

### Generative AI is multimodal

Modern generative systems are increasingly multimodal, meaning they can work across multiple type of data or modalities, such as text, images, audio, and video, and combine those signals in a single workflow. This ability unlocks richer interactions, such as describing a chart from a photo, generating an image from a written concept, or turning meeting audio into action-oriented notes.

> **Multimodal AI** creates rich new content from diverse sources and data types, including text, images, audio, and video.

Common multimodal patterns include:

- Text to image, such as image or video visuals from descriptions
- Text to audio, such as voice-overs, music, and AI-generated podcasts
- Image to text, such as captions or explanations
- Audio to text, such as transcripts and summaries
- Video to text, such as scene detection and storyboard drafts

These multimodal possibilities augment creative possibilities and accessibility, but also raise considerations around permissions, privacy, and bias. The responsible use of AI includes reviewing outputs, crediting sources where appropriate, and clearly indicating when generative AI created the content.

## What types of tasks can generative AI perform?

Generative AI excels when the goal is to create or reshape content. In practice, it supports four everyday types of tasks: content creation, problem solving, idea generation, and personalization, each using learned patterns to produce or adapt material. Recognizing these task types helps you choose effective prompts and decide how much human review or subject expertise the output requires.

### Content creation

Generative AI helps create content, including articles, emails, training materials, and product descriptions. It achieves this by using patterns learned from large datasets. Instead of writing each piece from scratch, teams use AI tools to generate and refine a strong first draft. This saves time and allows people to devote more energy to strategy and decision making.

For example, a marketing team might use an AI tool to draft hundreds of product descriptions for an online catalog, allowing them more time for planning campaigns.

### Problem solving

Generative AI is useful for solving problems that involve creativity or patterns. It helps teams develop various approaches to presenting ideas, structuring messages, or organizing materials. While it might not excel at solving complex logic problems or making decisions, it can help facilitate creative work by providing new options to consider.


For example, a communications team might ask an AI tool for different ways to explain a complex topic. Reviewing these drafts can help the team decide which version will be most clear for their audience.

### Idea generation

One of the most helpful ways to use generative AI is for idea generation. AI tools can help you get started when you feel stuck or want to explore new directions. Generative AI tools can suggest titles, themes, concepts, or questions to ask your audience. This ability makes it easier to get ideas on the page, allowing you to review and choose what works best.

For example, a project team might use AI to generate names for a new service. The tool can offer dozens of ideas in seconds, helping the team move forward with naming faster.

### Personalization

Generative AI can adjust content for different audiences. It can change the tone, style, or reading level to match people’s needs. This is particularly helpful when working with varied groups, especially if people learn in different ways or have varying levels of background knowledge.

For example, a learning team might use AI to rephrase technical training content using accessible language so that all employees can easily comprehend the material, regardless of their background knowledge.

## How generative AI is different from internet search

One of the easiest ways to start using generative AI is to prompt it with questions you would typically type into a search engine. What’s the best time to send a marketing email? How do you write a business case? What are some ideas for team-building activities?

Using generative AI feels similar to using a search engine, because you type in a prompt and receive a response. But generative AI operates quite differently from search engines.

*Select each tab to learn more about the differences between generative AI and search engines.*

### Search engines find existing content

**Search engines help you find information that already exists.** They scan and index billions of web pages, documents, and databases. Using indexing, search engines keep track of all the information they find on the internet. When a search engine scans a webpage, it saves key details, such as the page’s content, the words it contains, and its structure, in a database. So, when you search for something, the engine can quickly review the index to find matching results, instead of scanning the whole internet in real time.

Search engines rely on keywords, semantic tags, and ranking algorithms to determine the relevance of their results. These systems determine which results are most likely to match your query, based on how frequently a term appears on a page or how trustworthy the site appears. When you get a response from a search engine, you often get a small subset of all the available information on the web.

### Generative AI creates new content

**Generative AI creates new content based on patterns learned from massive datasets.** These datasets encompass a wide range of sources, including books, articles, websites, and other materials, across various subjects and formats. The content it produces is not copied from any one place. Instead, it reflects a broad understanding built during training.

Unlike search engines, a single response from a generative AI tool can draw from patterns found in billions of examples. This allows the tool to provide a helpful summary or fresh ideas without pointing to a specific source.

For example, a content creator might ask a generative AI tool to describe current industry trends. Instead of returning links, the tool produces a unique summary based on patterns across a wide range of data.

---

Search engines connect you to existing information, while generative AI allows you to create new content. Both tools are powerful, but they help with different challenges. Search engines are better when you need a direct source, citation, or recent update. Generative AI is more helpful when you need a starting point, a summary, or a creative response built from broad knowledge.

## You ask, AI creates

When you interact with generative AI tools, you need a way to tell the system what you want. The instructions or questions you give to AI are known as prompts.

> A **prompt** is the input statement, question, or instruction you provide to a generative AI system to guide it in creating a specific output.

Prompts are the connection between your ideas and the results you get from an AI tool. The more precise and detailed your prompt, the more likely you will receive accurate, helpful, and relevant responses. Learning how to craft effective prompts sets the foundation for successful communication with AI and helps you get better results.

The components of an effective AI prompt help you get more accurate, relevant, and helpful responses when using AI tools. Each component plays a role in guiding the technology so that the output aligns with your needs and expectations. To get the best results, include the following components in your prompt:

- Role: Defines who the AI should act as or what expertise it should use
- Context: Gives background information or sets the situation
- Task: Describes exactly what you want the AI to do
- Output: Explains what the result should look like (format or structure)
- Constraints: Sets any limits, such as length, tone, or required content

When you include each of these components in your prompts, you help the AI tool deliver more precise responses tailored to your goals.

## AI ethics implications: Generative AI

Generative AI technologies are transforming industries, but they also present AI ethics implications. Adhering to the Pillars of Trust and observing the responsible use of AI influences how these tools shape decisions, affect individuals, and impact society.

*Select each section to learn more about the AI ethics implications associated with generative AI.*

### Fairness

Generative AI creates new content based on patterns it has learned from training data. To promote **fairness**, developers train models on varied datasets and test outputs for signs of bias in how people, cultures, or perspectives are represented. If the data includes biased or unbalanced examples, the AI might generate outputs that reflect or amplify those patterns. Such outputs can lead to unfair treatment or misrepresentation of certain groups.

For example, an AI writing tool trained on unfamiliar media might describe job roles or leadership traits in ways that exclude cultural differences or reinforce stereotypes.

### Robustness

Generative AI can create false or misleading content that looks real, including deepfake videos, fake reviews, or impersonated text. To promote **robustness**, developers test systems against harmful prompts and include safeguards that prevent or identify dangerous use cases. It’s also necessary to design systems that are resistant to manipulation and misuse to minimize these risks.

For example, a malicious actor might use a generative AI image tool to create counterfeit product photos to purposefully damage a competitor’s brand. Strengthening a model’s handling of input helps reduce misuse and improve safety.

### Explainability

**Explainability** refers to the ability to understand and interpret the AI system’s decisions and actions. Generative AI systems do not always reveal how or why they produce a specific output, making it difficult for users to understand what influences a response or to correct inaccurate or inappropriate content. Because generative AI can create realistic text, images, and videos, it’s essential to explain when AI generates content and provide information about how the AI created the content.

For example, a company using generative AI to draft reports should clearly label the content and provide users with a way to verify key facts or sources. Understanding how the AI draws conclusions helps instill trust.

### Transparency

**Transparency** also includes sharing basic information about how a generative model was trained, the types of data used, and the tool’s intended purpose. When AI generates content, users need to know where it came from, how it was created, and what parts of it were produced by a machine. Labeling AI-generated text, images, or audio helps people recognize what was created by a machine and avoid confusion.

For example, an educational video that uses AI to generate narration or animations should include a note explaining which parts were AI-created. Disclosure helps users make informed choices when using or sharing generated content.

### Privacy

To protect **privacy**, training data should be anonymized and carefully filtered to provide confidentiality. Generative AI models train on large amounts of data and should be monitored to prevent the generation of content that reveals personal or confidential information. If that data includes personal information, there is a risk that the model might reproduce private or sensitive content without permission.

For example, an AI text generator might create a sentence that includes parts of a real person’s medical or financial records if that data was included in its training set.
