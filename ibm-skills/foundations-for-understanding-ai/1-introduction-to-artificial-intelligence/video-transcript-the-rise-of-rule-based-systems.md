# The rise of rule-based systems

Today’s AI is capable of learning and adapting. But that wasn’t always the case. Early attempts at building intelligent systems were simple, rule-based systems that followed strict instructions to help users solve problems. Understanding how rule-based systems function can help you unravel how AI has evolved.

A rule-based system makes decisions using fixed rules. A fixed rule is an explicit command statement, for example, “Request a user’s account balance from the database and display it on the screen”. You might think of these rules as a list of instructions, such as a recipe in a cookbook.

Each rule tells the system exactly how to behave in specific situations. For example, if a scientist needed a calculation for the movement of a planet, they would input numbers into a computer, which would perform the calculation and output the result.

While rule-based systems are adequate for simple, repetitive tasks, they do have significant performance limitations. They can’t learn from new information or adapt to unexpected situations. If a problem falls outside their rules, they get stuck. This makes them rigid and difficult to use for complex or challenging tasks, such as understanding speech or recognizing images.

To handle more complex problems, researchers began developing branching logic. A branching logic solves problems using a sequence of logical steps that uses goals and input to determine how to proceed. A branching program is a set of instructions that tells the system how to reach a goal by working through possible options. For example, a system might search through different moves to win a game or solve a math equation.

These systems didn’t just respond to inputs like rule-based systems. Instead, they could explore different paths and make decisions based on goals as well as inputs. Branching logic offered more flexibility than rule-based systems, but still couldn’t learn from data or adapt to new information on their own.

As computers became faster and data increased, it became clear that rule-based and basic branching systems had limitations. While these systems are valuable in many contexts, certain problems require more adaptive approaches. They couldn’t handle complex tasks such as recognizing images, understanding speech, or making personalized recommendations.

This need led to the rise of machine learning, where computers can learn from data instead of following only fixed rules. It was this shift that made AI more flexible and powerful.

Rule-based and branching systems laid the foundation for today’s AI. They demonstrated how computers could follow logic, make decisions, and automate tasks. And they highlighted the need for systems that can learn and adapt. The history of early AI reveals how far AI has advanced and why adaptability and pattern recognition are now so significant.
