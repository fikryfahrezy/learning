# AI in action from vision to decision

Artificial intelligence is all around you, quietly working in the background to make life easier and more interesting. From helping your phone recognize your face to suggesting your next favorite song, AI has the capabilities to understand, offer insights, and create.

AI doesn’t just power big systems; it transforms your everyday life at home and work. Take Martin, for example. He’s a content coordinator for a growing media company. Martin creates video clips, writes summaries, and selects visuals for the company’s newsletter and social media. In this fast-paced role,
balancing creativity with repetitive tasks can be challenging.

With tight deadlines and a growing list of deliverables, Martin spends hours editing previews, writing blurbs, choosing thumbnails, and guessing which content will perform well. The process is timeconsuming, repetitive, and often involves trial and error.

Martin’s team has introduced a new creative AI assistant. This AI-powered assistant supports Martin’s daily workflow, helping to organize, create, and deliver content. Let’s follow along as Martin begins using the planner.

First, Martin opens the tool’s task planner. The task planner relies on decision-making AI, a form of AI that evaluates available options and context to guide users. The task planner considers deadlines, complexity, and priorities to suggest an efficient task order. Martin uses the recommended workflow to organize his week and reduce decision fatigue.

Then, Martin uploads several long video clips from last weekend’s conference. The system uses computer vision, a form of AI that helps computers understand images and video. It checks each frame to find the clearest, best-lit, and most balanced shots. To do this, the AI uses techniques such as edge detection to identify shapes and brightness analysis to measure lighting. It also examines how things are
arranged in the frame, similar to how a photographer thinks about framing a picture. Instead of watching hours of footage, Martin gets a short list of top clips to choose from.

Next, Martin writes summaries and social media captions based on the short clips. He uses generative AI, a form of AI that creates new content based on patterns it learned from existing examples. Martin types in a few keywords, and the generative AI drafts professional, brand-aligned copy for his review.

Before finalizing the content, Martin checks the platform’s analytics tool. The analytics tool uses predictive AI, a form of AI that offers insights and recommendations based on patterns it identifies. Based on past performance, it recommends video formats and post times. Martin uses this information to schedule posts strategically.

With the support of AI tools, Martin confidently and quickly completes projects. The video clips are polished, the captions are strong, and the schedule reflects real performance insights. Freed from repetitive tasks, Martin has more time to focus on creative strategy and team collaboration. 

By using different forms of AI, Martin improved his work quality and made better use of his time. The AI tools didn’t replace his creativity; they supported it. The AI assistant helped him work smarter and focus on what creates the most impact. Across roles and industries, AI is steadily becoming part of daily work.
