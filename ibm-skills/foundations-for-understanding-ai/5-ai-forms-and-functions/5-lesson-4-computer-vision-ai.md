## What is computer vision AI?

As you explore how artificial intelligence tools process information, you’ll encounter technologies that help machines analyze visual data. One of these technologies is computer vision.

> **Computer vision AI** is a technology that enables machines to interpret and recognize visual information from digital images and videos using algorithms and pattern recognition.

Computer vision AI extracts useful information from photos, videos, and other visual inputs. Unlike human vision, which relies on biological senses and experience, computer vision uses mathematical models and large datasets to identify objects, patterns, and details. This technology forms the foundation for many AI-powered applications, ranging from facial recognition to autonomous vehicles.

For example, a computer vision system in a retail store can automatically track inventory by analyzing images from security cameras, helping store managers know when shelves need restocking.

Every day, cameras and smartphones capture images that different apps can recognize or sort. This ability relies on computer vision, which enables computers to process and identify visual information by converting raw image data into practical actions or decisions.

*Select each marker to explore more about how computers process and analyze images.*

### Image capture

A device, such as a camera, collects a digital picture, converting what you see into a grid of tiny colored dots called pixels. This raw image becomes the starting point for computer analysis.

### Image preprocessing

The system improves the image by removing blur, adjusting colors, or sharpening details, making it easier to spot relevant parts later. For example, it might brighten a dark photo, allowing features to stand out.

### Feature detection

The technology searches the image for key details, such as edges, corners, or unique patterns, which help describe what is present. Finding these features allows the system to focus on what matters most.

### Pattern recognition

The computer compares the detected features to a large set of examples, looking for matches that suggest what the image contains. This step helps the system distinguish between a stop sign and a tree, or a person and a pet.

### Decision output

Based on its findings, the system produces a result, such as tagging an object, sending an alert, or unlocking a phone. This final step turns image analysis into a practical action or answer.

## Practical uses of computer vision AI

Computer vision technology transforms how people interact with the world by enabling machines to analyze and interpret visual information. This technology supports a range of practical solutions, from identifying objects in everyday life to enhancing creative projects and improving accessibility for individuals with visual impairments.

*Select each section to learn more about the practical uses of computer vision technology.*

### Identifying objects

**Object identification** tools use computer vision to identify and label objects in photos or videos. These tools enable people to quickly identify plants, products, or parts in a warehouse. By providing instant feedback, they support better decision-making and reduce the time spent searching for information. This technology brings expert knowledge to anyone with a camera-enabled device. 

For example, a warehouse worker uses a mobile app to scan a barcode or take a photo of a product, instantly receiving information about its location and handling instructions. 

### Enhancing content with new graphics

Content creation tools powered by computer vision help users **enhance content with new graphics**. These tools can produce images and graphics more efficiently, streamlining creative processes and enabling teams to focus on strategy and innovation rather than manual tasks. This technology helps businesses boost productivity and deliver high-quality visual content.

For example, a marketing team uses an AI tool to automatically generate branded social media images from product photos, thereby saving hours of manual design work.

### Assisting vision-impaired users

Accessibility enhancements use computer vision to **assist vision-impaired users** or other disabilities. These tools can describe scenes, read text aloud, or help users navigate unfamiliar spaces. By making digital and physical environments more accessible, this technology promotes independence and inclusion, thereby opening up new opportunities for everyone to engage with the world.

For example, an employee with low vision uses a smartphone app that reads printed documents aloud during meetings, enabling full participation without requiring additional assistance.

## AI ethics implications: Computer vision AI

Computer vision tools are a part of everyday life, from unlocking phones with facial recognition to analyzing images in healthcare and security applications. As these systems become increasingly powerful, developers must address the AI ethics implications associated with developing and using computer vision systems.

Imagine a city’s marketing team is developing a new computer vision app for their annual art festival. The app will help visitors explore public art by identifying sculptures and murals and providing fun facts about each piece. Building such an app means making careful choices to promote the responsible use of AI.

*Select each section to learn more about the AI ethics implications associated with computer vision AI.*

### Fairness

**Fairness** means achieving accurate artwork recognition regardless of the artwork’s origin or creator. If the training images primarily feature a specific artist or style, the app might miss or mislabel works from other backgrounds. The app should recognize art from many artists, cultures, and styles, accurately labeling everyone’s artwork.

### Robustness

**Robustness** means the app can correctly identify art, even when things aren’t perfect or someone tries to fool it. The app needs to resist tricks, such as someone placing a sticker on a mural to confuse the system. It must also function properly even when photos have been shot from unusual angles, using poor lighting, or with crowds in the background.

### Explainability

**Explainability** enables the app to clearly communicate the features it uses, making its responses easy to understand for both festival visitors and organizers. When the app identifies a sculpture, users might want to know how it arrived at that decision. Was it the color, the shape, or the style?

### Transparency

**Transparency** fosters trust, so users need to know how the app is analyzing images. The app should specify when it’s using the camera and explain what data it collects and why, allowing festival goers to know how photos are being used.

### Privacy

The app should protect users’ **privacy** by only collecting images necessary for art recognition, not storing personal information, while also providing users with easy control and deletion options for their photos. Privacy means visitors can use the app, confident that their data is safe and not being misused.
