# Lesson 1: Can Computer Think

## Can a computer think?

Recall the last time a friend asked you for a restaurant recommendation. Immediately, you started thinking of memories of past meals, knowledge of your friend’s taste, and a list of places in the city. You were able to make a quick judgment about which spot would delight them.

Computers don’t think in the same way humans do. Instead of relying on feelings or personal experiences, computers process large amounts of information using mathematical rules and algorithms. While AI systems don’t possess consciousness or emotions, they can behave in ways that appear intelligent.

Learning how artificial intelligence works helps to understand how humans and computers process information. It’s helpful to examine two key ideas: thinking and intelligence.

> **Thinking** is the process of using information to form ideas, solve problems, or make decisions.

Thinking is the process by which you make sense of the world. Humans think by combining past experiences, emotions, and knowledge to understand situations and make choices. AI systems also “think” in a more limited way. They collect data, analyze it, identify patterns, and select an output. Now let’s see how those thinking steps are applied to AI systems.

## How AI “thinks”

Exploring how AI systems think helps you understand their impact and potential. Analyzing how AI mimics human thinking, from gathering data to producing results, reveals the strengths and limits of these technologies.

*Read the following to learn more about how AI systems think to learn and improve.*

### Step 1: Input recognition

AI systems begin by receiving input. AI systems can gather input from various sources, including sensors, cameras, websites, and databases. This data can include numbers, text, images, or sounds. The quality and variety of the data determine how well the AI can make conclusions.

### Step 2: Pattern analysis

In pattern analysis, AI identifies trends, relationships, or recurring features in the input. Using algorithms, AI identifies connections that might not be apparent to humans. This step enables AI to recognize what is normal, detect changes, and identify important details. Effective pattern analysis enables AI to interpret complex information and inform decision making.

### Step 3: Decision logic

Decision logic is the process AI uses to determine what action to take, based on identified patterns, and guided by models and predefined rules. These rules involve probabilities and learned experience. Decision logic helps AI systems respond quickly and consistently to new situations.

### Step 4: Output generation

Output generation occurs when the AI system produces a result, such as an insight, recommendation, or new content. This output is based on the data, patterns, and decisions made in earlier steps. The output can be a simple answer, a report, or even an image or piece of text. Clear output helps people make better decisions or take action quickly.

---

While thinking involves processing information, intelligence enables humans and machines to learn from that information and improve over time.

> **Intelligence** is the ability to learn, understand, and solve problems using knowledge and reasoning.

Understanding your own thinking and intelligence can help you appreciate what AI does. As a human, you take in information, solve problems, and adjust when something changes. You use your past experiences to make decisions and learn from mistakes. This is how human thinking and intelligence work.

AI systems also process information and generate outputs to assist with decision making. But they do not think the way you do. They do not feel or reflect. They follow patterns in data and adjust based on what they are trained to recognize.

## Learning

You might have tried and fallen a few times when you first learned to ride a bike. You might have learned by watching a parent or older sibling ride their bike. Over time, you gradually got better at riding a bike by paying attention to what worked and didn’t.

> **Learning** is the process of gaining new knowledge, skills, or understanding through experience, study, or instruction.

With learning defined, let’s examine how learning happens in people and machines.

### How learning happens

Learning is a process that helps both humans and machines improve over time. While the tools and methods differ, many learning methods follow similar patterns.

*Select each marker to explore how humans learn.*

#### Memory

Learning requires storing and recalling information. People remember facts, events, and emotions. They build connections over time based on their experiences. 

### Imitation

People often learn by observing and copying others, repeating phrases, or modeling behaviors.

### Symbology and association

Humans learn to associate words, images, or objects with meaning, such as recognizing that a red light signifies “stop”. This involves context, experience, and cultural understanding.

### Emotion recognition

Humans use emotional cues to support learning. Emotions such as curiosity, frustration, or excitement help signal what to pay attention to, what to avoid, and when to keep trying.

### Trial and error

Humans respond to praise, correction, or outcomes that show success or failure. They adjust their behavior over time to improve based on trial and error.

### Data exposure

Learning begins with exposure to information. People gather knowledge through experiences, conversations, reading, and observation. This initial input provides the raw material for learning to begin.

These methods illustrate how humans learn and improve over time. Now, let’s explore how computers use similar methods to learn from data and improve over time.

## How computers learn

Computers learn over time by analyzing information, finding patterns, and continually refining their behavior. Their learning process is guided by key stages that demonstrate how machines transform raw data into informed decisions.

### Data Exposure

Learning begins with exposure to data. For AI systems, this means receiving large volumes of input that represent examples of what the system is expected to learn. AI systems don’t explore or seek information the way humans do. Instead, they are trained on data that is prepared and input in a structured way.

### Pattern discovery

Once exposed to data, AI uses algorithms to detect patterns. These patterns could be visual features, word pairings, data trends, or other measurable relationships. Over time, the system begins to associate specific patterns with specific outcomes, much like humans learn from imitation. 

Patterns aren’t meaningful to AI the way they are to humans. The system does not identify “a cat” because it knows what a cat is. It identifies pixel arrangements that statistically correlate with the label “cat”.

### Rule adjusment

AI systems improve their performance by adjusting internal parameters based on feedback. The system is tested during training to see whether its insights are correct. When the insights are incorrect, the system adjusts the strength of its internal connections to reduce the error. These adjustments accumulate into a “memory” of what works best.

### Feedback implementation

AI systems receive feedback by comparing their predictions to a correct answer and using the difference to adjust. This process repeats thousands or millions of times during training. Eventually, the AI becomes better at providing insights, even when presented with slightly unfamiliar data. 

Some AI systems continue learning after deployment, especially if designed to update based on user behavior. Others stop learning once training is complete and only apply what they already know.

Learning produces knowledge. But knowledge alone doesn’t solve problems. What makes learning and knowledge useful is the ability to apply them. Let’s explore how humans and computers use what they’ve learned to make sense of new situations, consider their existing knowledge, and determine the next course of action.

## Reasoning

Have you ever made dinner only to realize you’re out of a key ingredient? The recipe calls for sour cream, but there isn’t any in the fridge. Instead of giving up, you stop and think. What have you used before? Would yogurt work? What about cream cheese?

You think about what flavors go together, what you’ve used in other recipes, and what makes the most sense for your meal. There’s no exact rule to follow. You’re using what you already know to solve a new problem. That process is called reasoning.

> **Reasoning** is the mental process of applying knowledge and logic to understand information, solve problems, and make informed decisions. Reasoning involves thinking critically, analyzing ideas, and drawing conclusions based on knowledge and experience.

Reasoning helps people and machines make sense of the world, but their approaches to reasoning differ.

Human reasoning draws from many sources. People think through situations using logic, past experiences, emotions, and personal judgment. For example, deciding whether to change jobs might be based on factors such as salary, commute, personal values, and how the new role aligns with one’s goals.

Machines reason differently. Instead, AI systems follow rules, recognize patterns in data, and use math to make decisions. A system that detects credit card fraud does not “know” what fraud is. It compares transactions to past examples and flags anything unusual based on what it has been trained to recognize.

Both humans and machines solve problems and make choices, but they do so in different ways.

*Select each section to learn more about the core differences between human and machine reasoning.*

### Emotionality

Emotions, instincts, and personal values often influence human reasoning. People might decide based on how something feels, even if the facts suggest another option. Emotions such as fear, excitement, or empathy can shape how problems are perceived and which outcomes are preferred.

AI systems, conversely, do not feel. Instead, AI relies on probability, pattern recognition, and statistical relationships learned from data.

Understanding this difference is important. Human reasoning might be unpredictable or biased, but it allows for empathy, intuition, and moral judgment. AI is more consistent and objective, but it can’t account for the emotional layers that often matter most in complex decisions.

### Tranparency

Human reasoning is often hidden. People make decisions based on thoughts, feelings, experiences, and instincts that might not be fully explained, even to themselves. It’s not always clear why someone chooses one option over another. Memory, mood, bias, or habit can all influence reasoning in ways that are difficult to identify.

AI reasoning is more transparent, but not always easy to understand. Many AI systems follow defined steps, calculations, or models that can be documented or reviewed. In theory, it is possible to track how a machine reaches a decision. You can sometimes find out what data it used, what patterns it recognized, and what rules it applied. This makes some AI systems more explainable than human decisions.

Not all AI models are simple. Large language models and deep learning systems often rely on complex mechanisms, producing results that are difficult to interpret.

### Contextuality

Humans reason with an awareness of context. When making decisions, people consider time, place, relationships, tone, culture, and experience. A single action might be understood differently depending on who is involved, what has happened previously, or what the broader situation requires. This ability to adjust thinking based on context is a core strength of human reasoning.

Although AI systems can be trained to recognize patterns that often appear in certain situations, they don’t use context to process information. AI treats context as data, something to be labeled, tokenized, or matched, not something to be felt or interpreted. 

This matters because many decisions require more than simple pattern matching. Human reasoning can adapt to nuance, shift based on tone, or consider social dynamics. AI systems excel when the task is well defined but can fail when meaning depends on subtle or unstated context.

### Adaptivity

Human reasoning is naturally adaptive. People can change their thinking when circumstances shift, rules no longer apply, or new information appears. Humans can improvise, ask questions, or draw connections in unfamiliar situations, even when their knowledge is limited. This flexibility enables people to handle edge cases, solve novel problems, and adjust their decisions as needed.

AI systems are adaptive within limits. They can improve over time through training, feedback, or prompt engineering, but they do not adapt in the moment the way humans do. A machine’s ability to adjust depends on how it was built, what it was trained on, and how it is prompted. Some systems can generate creative responses or appear flexible, but the underlying logic still follows patterns shaped by data, not intuition or lived experience.

Adaptivity matters because not every situation has a clear answer. While AI can generate useful outputs across many tasks, human reasoning remains better suited for navigating uncertainty, exceptions, and ambiguous areas where no rule or pattern fits perfectly.

### Consistency

Machine reasoning is strong in consistency and explainability. Machines always follow the same process, which leads to reliable and repeatable results. They can also explain their choices clearly, showing exactly which data and rules were used. In contrast, human decisions might vary based on mood, context, or external influences, making them more difficult to predict or explain.

### Part 1: How a human food expert learns

#### Question 1: Recalling a family dish

Chef Nia glances at a photo of a dish. It’s a bowl of rice. She opens another photo, and it’s a jar of curry. She pauses, then smiles: “My aunt made a delicious lamb korma all the time. I know what I’ll make.” She thinks back to the aroma, the creamy texture, and the cinnamon she once tasted in it. Without needing a recipe, she starts jotting down the steps.

Which form of learning is Chef Nia using?

- Memory
- Symbology and association
- Emotion recognition
- Trial and error

**Answer: Memory**

Chef Nia uses memory to recall past experiences and sensory details, which helps her decide what to prepare.

#### Question 2: Reacting to a caramel photo

Chef Nia opens a magazine showing a photo of a flowing, golden caramel sauce. As soon as she sees it, she thinks about the first time she made flan. “I remember how proud I felt when I finally got the texture of flan right.” She decides to create a recipe for a unique flan.

Which form of learning is Chef Nia using?

- Emotion recognition
- Memory
- Symbology and association
- Trial and error

**Answer: Emotion recognition**

Chef Nia’s choice is guided by how she feels when she sees the image. This is an example of emotion recognition, where emotions influence what humans focus on and remember.

#### Question 3: Perfecting a brownie recipe

Chef Nia sees a photo of a pantry with cocoa powder and a half-used bag of sugar. A photo of a refrigerator shows eggs and butter. “It looks like we’ve got the basics for brownies”, she says. She creates a recipe, but the first batch turns out dry. She adjusts the butter and baking time, makes another attempt, and perfects the gooey texture.

Which form of learning is Chef Nia using?

- Trial and error
- Memory
- Symbology and association
- Emotional recognition

**Answer: Trial and error**

Chef Nia is using a trial-and-error approach by testing a recipe, evaluating the results, and adjusting her approach to improve the outcome.
