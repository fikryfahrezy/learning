# From if-then to intelligence

Like obedient robots, early computers followed precise instructions. If you wanted a computer program to add a set of numbers, you’d give it both the numbers and the formula, and it would give you the answer. These systems worked using fixed rules, and forgetting even one detail would result in inaccurate results.

While early computer systems were designed to imitate human thinking, they were largely deterministic; the logic that produced their output was fixed. The same input always resulted in the same output, and these systems couldn’t learn or adapt over time.

Think about using if-then rules to block spam emails. A programmer might have written, “If an email says, ‘FREE MONEY’, then it’s spam.” But spam writers got sneaky and began writing “FR33 M0N3Y” instead, so the programmers had to repeatedly update the if-then rules. This cycle forced programmers to adapt to every trick.

Algorithmic if-then systems captured what human experts knew: there was a big problem. So,
programmers had to think ahead for every possible situation, which is very difficult in the real world.

Machine learning brought big changes. Instead of writing complex rules, developers designed AI systems to learn on their own. By analyzing thousands of spam and valid emails, AI could find patterns, such as excessive use of capital letters or emails from odd addresses, on its own.

Movie streaming services are a great example of how systems can learn. Instead of programming rules for each viewer’s likes and dislikes, these systems use programs to learn about preferences from millions of users with similar viewing habits. Their recommendation software identifies patterns that would be challenging for humans to find.

While these early learning machines were very powerful, they had limits, too. They couldn’t handle complicated data such as pictures, voices, or language very well.

Now, neural networks, which work more like the human brain and can handle complicated data, are helping. They examine small bits of data at a time and make recommendations and forecast likely outcomes based on what they calculate. This method is called layering.

When examining a photo, neural networks break down image data into layers. The first layer might find the photo’s edges. The next layer forms shapes using the edges. Deeper layers recognize whole objects.

This layered approach made AI much more capable. Neural networks are designed to handle complex tasks that basic if-then rules never could.

Deep learning advanced the potential of neural networks. This type of AI uses many layers, sometimes hundreds, and large datasets to help solve complex problems. 

AI translation services are an excellent example of applying deep learning to real-world problems. Early translation programs used fixed rules and generated poor results. Deep learning systems learn by reading millions of documents in multiple languages, learning grammar and how different languages expressed ideas.

These improvements are already a part of your daily lives and routines. Voice assistants understand natural speech, phone cameras can automatically enhance photos, and apps suggest what you might like based on past behavior.

The change from rigid if-then rules to today’s AI systems is a significant shift in technology. AI systems are beginning to help solve problems in ways even their creators didn’t expect.
