# Lesson 2: Machine Learning and Hard Problem

## How AI approaches problems

AI systems help people solve problems by processing large amounts of information quickly and accurately.

Artificial intelligence uses algorithms to sort, calculate, and process data, enabling people to make informed, structured decisions in healthcare, education, and retail. By recognizing patterns in large datasets, AI identifies trends and detects unusual activity.

AI uses decision making to select the most effective actions based on the information it receives. For example, virtual assistants suggest calendar appointments, businesses automate inventory orders, and schools use innovative tools to schedule classes.

Machine learning models enable AI to continually improve over time by learning from feedback, enhancing performance in tasks such as tracking fitness goals, grading student work, or managing business logistics.

Understanding how AI processes information is key to evaluating its impact on fairness, privacy, and transparency in automated decisions.

## How computers can help with the hardest problems

Many of today’s challenges require quick processing of huge amounts of information.

AI systems rapidly sort through and analyze vast amounts of data, making it possible to find answers and patterns much faster than humans can. The ability of AI systems to scale means the systems can handle problems that are too big or complex for people to solve alone, such as forecasting severe weather events or tracking the spread of diseases.

After exploring how machines learn from data to solve complex problems, similar to how people learn from experience, let’s investigate how these learning methods work in real-world situations, from recommending products online to helping doctors make more informed decisions.

## Features of AI systems used in solving problems

Modern artificial intelligence machines possess unique features that enable them to process vast amounts of information and identify patterns. Understanding these features helps reveal how AI systems deliver results that impact many aspects of daily life.

*Read the following to learn more about how the features of AI systems help solve problems.*

### Speed of computation

Speed of computation refers to how quickly a machine can process and analyze information. Powerful processors enable AI systems to perform billions of calculations every second. This rapid processing is essential for handling complex tasks.

For example, AI-powered medical devices can quickly scan and interpret X-ray images, enabling doctors to diagnose patients within seconds.

### Handling large datasets

Handling large datasets enables machines to efficiently store, organize, and analyze vast amounts of information from multiple sources. AI systems rely on this ability to find meaningful insights in data that would overwhelm a human. 

For example, a retail company uses AI to analyze millions of customer transactions, providing valuable insights into shopping trends.

### Scalability across systems

Scalability across systems means AI solutions can expand and function smoothly on different platforms, industries, or environments without losing performance. This flexibility allows organizations to apply AI to new challenges as their needs grow. Scalable systems can support more users, larger datasets, or additional features without slowing down or requiring a complete rebuild.

For example, a logistics company can use the same AI route-planning tool for both a small fleet and a global network of delivery trucks.

### Real-time adjustment

Real-time adjustment refers to the ability of AI systems to update their outputs instantly as new data becomes available. This feature helps machines respond to changing conditions and make decisions as needed. Real-time adjustments are necessary in dynamic environments where situations can change rapidly. 

For example, navigation apps use AI to reroute drivers instantly when they detect traffic jams or road closures.

## Humans and computers solving problems together

Artificial intelligence is most effective when it works with people, combining the strengths of human judgment and machine processing. In many workplaces, technology supports decision making, uncovers valuable insights, and sparks new ideas, but it still relies on human expertise to guide and validate its outputs.

*Select each section to learn more about how humans and AI work together to enhance performance and decision making.*

### Guiding and validating outputs

Humans play a significant role in reviewing and refining the output of AI systems. While AI can generate reports, summaries, or recommendations, people must verify these outputs for accuracy, relevance, and alignment with business goals. This process helps identify errors, enhance understanding, and deliver results that meet real-world needs. By guiding and validating AI outputs, organizations ensure that technology supports, rather than replaces, human expertise.

### Support human tasks

AI tools can assist humans by handling time-consuming or complex tasks. For example, generative AI can summarize documents, draft communications, or suggest solutions based on past data. These systems offer speed, consistency, and scale that humans alone can’t match. By using AI to automate routine tasks and surface valuable insights, people can focus on critical thinking, creativity, and informed decision making.

### Cocreating solutions

When people and AI systems collaborate through prompting, iteration, and feedback, they can cocreate innovative ideas and solutions. For example, a human might prompt an AI to generate social media marketing post ideas, then revise the result based on tone or audience fit. This back-and-forth process blends machine-generated options with human judgment.

### Assistin decision making

AI can help people weigh options by providing insights, risk assessments, or detailed comparisons, enabling them to make informed decisions. Humans use this information to choose the most effective course of action that aligns with their goals and values. While AI provides data-driven insights, people ultimately make the final decisions, considering factors that technology might overlook.

## Ethical implications

As AI increasingly becomes part of both work and personal life, you must consider the impact of these systems on individuals and society. This is the discipline called AI ethics.

> **AI ethics** refers to a multidisciplinary field that studies how to optimize AI’s beneficial impact while reducing risks and adverse outcomes.

Understanding AI ethics helps you recognize why it matters to encourage fair and transparent behavior in AI systems. When you’re a user or designer of AI systems, thinking about ethics means considering how AI’s output affects others.

Imagine what goes into building a skyscraper so that it doesn’t fall, even in dramatic events such as an earthquake. Every floor must rest on a solid foundation, or the whole structure is at risk. The same is true for artificial intelligence. IBM developed five pillars to guide the responsible adoption of technologies, including AI. These five pillars are intended to address the issue of trust.

*Select each card to learn more about IBM’s five Pillars of Trust.*

- **Explainability** refers to thes ability to understand and interpret the decisions and actions of AI systems.Knowing the steps that led to an outcome instills trust. For example, understanding why a medical AI suggested one treatment over another helps doctors feel confident in using the technology.
- **Fairness** means AI should treat individuals or groups equally. When properly calibrated, AI can assist humans in making fairer choices and countering biases. This leads to outcomes that benefit people from all backgrounds. For example, a hiring AI that is fair will not favor one group over another, helping companies build diverse and inclusive teams.
- **Robustness** refers to an AIS system's ability to perform reliably and safely, even in unexpected conditions or attacks. Robust systems are designed to resist manipulation, security breaches, or input meant to confuse or trick them. For example, robust Al in a self-driving car stays effective even if someone tries to interfere with traffic signs or sensors.
- **Transparency** means being open about how an AI system is built, trained, and makes decisions. When you know how an Al service works, you can hold it accountable and trust the results. For example, transparency in a loan approval AI lets banks and customers understand why a loan was approved or denied.
- **Privacy** means keeping your data safe and ensuring that AI systems adhere to rules governing the use of data. This protects you from misuse or information leaks. For example, a health app that keeps your medical data private follows the privacy pillar and builds trust with users.

You have just explored the five Pillars of Trust. Together, these principles guide the development of AI systems that promote trust.
