# Lesson 2: Form Data Problems to Capabilities

## From data problems to AI capabilities

Every day, you use information to make decisions. Whether you’re choosing what to eat for lunch, figuring out how much money you’ve spent, or texting a friend, you’re working with information.  

As the amount and complexity of information increased, humans invented tools, from pencil and paper to powerful computers, to help with these tasks. Today, you can use AI not only to retrieve and store data, but also to interpret it, learn from it, and take action to solve problems and inform decisions with greater speed and accuracy.

### How humans work with information

Humans rely on several core data functions to navigate daily life and solve problems. These functions shape the way information is processed, stored, and used, and play a role in how people understand the world and make decisions.

*Select each tab to learn more about fundamental human data functions.*

#### Counting

Counting is one of the oldest ways people work with information. It helps answer the question, “How many?” A shepherd might have counted sheep using pebbles, dropping one into a pouch for each animal in the field. 

Later, tools such as calculators and spreadsheets made it easier to quickly and accurately count things. A store manager, for example, can enter sales data into a spreadsheet to track the number of items sold.

Today, AI makes counting even more powerful. A sensor at a school entrance uses computer vision to count the number of students entering each morning. That data might help staff track attendance in real time. Counting might seem simple, but it’s the foundation for bigger decisions.

#### Recording and recalling

Recording helps people keep track of important information, so it doesn’t get forgotten or lost. Thousands of years ago, someone might have recorded the number of grain sacks in a village using marks on clay tablets.

As the world became more complex, so did the recording tools. For instance, a doctor might use a computer to record patient notes in a medical database, making it easier to track a patient's health over time and share updates with other healthcare professionals. 

AI enhances recording by automating tasks. During a business meeting, an AI assistant can listen to the conversation, summarize the discussion, and highlight the key decisions made.

#### Analyzing and deciding

Analyzing occurs when people take raw data and find meaning in it. It’s how patterns are discovered and decisions are improved. A farmer, for example, might have once looked at the sky and soil to decide when to plant crops, analyzing signs from nature through experience.

With the rise of computers, analysis became more data-driven. For example, a coach might use statistics from multiple games to determine which players perform best under pressure.

Today, AI processes vast amounts of data in seconds. An AI tool can scan thousands of product reviews to determine what customers like or dislike. This insight helps companies improve their products.

#### Recognizing patterns

Recognition is about knowing what something is, based on experience or familiar patterns. A person might recognize friends in the distance by the way they walk or laugh. This kind of recognition is quick, often happening without much thought.

Computers recognize patterns, too. For example, a simple email filter recognizes a phrase such as “Congratulations, you’ve won!” and moves those emails to the spam folder. The email filter follows a set of rules that tell it what to look for.

AI brings recognition to a whole new level. Think about a smart car that recognizes a stop sign, a red light, or a pedestrian stepping into the road. The smart car system must make split-second decisions based on what it detects. 

#### Communicating and translating

Communication is how people exchange ideas with others, allowing information to move from one person, team, or place to another. Long ago, this might have been drawing on a cave wall to show the location of animals or sharing a story verbally. 

Later, tools such as printing presses, telephones, and email helped people communicate more quickly and over longer distances. A teacher might create a presentation to help students understand a science topic. 

Today, AI helps people communicate in new and exciting ways. A student learning a new language might use an AI-powered app that gives real-time feedback on pronunciation. Creative communication is evolving with generative AI. These AI tools help create stories, design posters, and generate music.

--- 

The fundamental human data functions, including counting, recording, analyzing, recognizing patterns, and communicating, form the foundation of how humans process and use information.

## The three eras of processing data

Humans have always used fundamental data functions to collect, process, and use data to support informed decision making. Over time, the methods for interacting with data have changed significantly. Understanding how these approaches have evolved provides context for how current systems, including artificial intelligence, fit into the broader picture of problem-solving.

*Read the following to learn more about the three eras of processing data and discover what makes them unique.*

### The Era of Tabulation

The **Era of Tabulation** introduced the use of machines to structure data and reveal hidden insights. For centuries, humans struggled to extract meaning from large amounts of unstructured (images, audio, text) data. To organize data, humans began developing machines. For example, over 2,000 years ago, Chinese tax collectors used the abacus to sort tax receipts, which aided their decisions on the construction of the Great Wall.

### The Era of Programming

The **Era of Programming** marked a breakthrough in data processing as scientists developed electronic computers that could run multiple instructions or “programs”. In 1946, the University of Pennsylvania introduced the revolutionary Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer (ENIAC). The applications for ENIAC included atomic energy calculations, ballistics, random number studies, thermal ignition, weather forecasts, and wind tunnel design. ENIAC could perform calculations in 30 seconds that previously took 12 hours using handheld calculators. In 1960, IBM introduced their first supercomputer, the IBM 7030. The IBM 7030 performed calculations in one day that took even the world’s fastest computers six months to solve! Today, the Era of Programming continues, with applications ranging from smartphones to space exploration, all powered by computer programming.

### The Era of AI

The **Era of AI** began in 1956 when John McCarthy and Marvin Minsky, along with a small group of researchers, coined the term “artificial intelligence” at Dartmouth College. They proposed that machines could simulate human learning and intelligence. By the mid-1990s, advances in computing power enabled machines to solve more complex problems. Today, the Era of AI continues, with AI facilitating advancements in fields including cancer research, big data analysis, and energy production.

--- 

Data processing has evolved from manual counting to using advanced artificial intelligence. Each era built on the previous one, demonstrating how new tools and ideas enable faster and more accurate management of information.

## Evolving tools for evolving needs

The way people work with data has dramatically transformed over time, from simple recording to advanced analysis. Early tools focused on preserving information, but as data volume grew, the analysis process became overwhelming. This shift from passive storage to active analysis laid the foundation for modern AI systems, which now use algorithms to identify patterns, offer insights about outcomes, and automate decision-making processes.

> An **algorithm** is a set of rules or a step-by-step procedure that guides machines to perform a task or solve a problem.

## From programming to AI: Stages in AI development

Artificial intelligence is rapidly advancing, building on the innovations of cell phones, personal computers, software, and the internet. From early thinking machines to today’s smart learning systems, AI has evolved through several stages to shape the way people use technology today.

*Select each marker to explore the stages in the development of artificial intelligence.*

### Thinking machines

Inventors and scientists imagined devices that might reason and solve problems, promoting the first efforts to create intelligent technology. The Turing Machine, developed by Alan Turing in the 1930s, laid the groundwork for machines that can solve problems and perform computations.

### Fixed-program systems

Engineers designed computers that followed detailed sets of instructions, but these systems could not adapt or handle new situations. Early calculators or tabulating machines were programmed to perform specific, repetitive tasks, such as counting or sorting, but could not adapt to new or complex tasks.

### Learning machines

Researchers developed systems that identify patterns and learn from historical data, enabling them to improve over time. This stage enabled machines to recognize speech, identify images, or recommend music choices based on user preferences. Examples of learning machines are recommendation engines, in products such as Netflix or Spotify. These systems learn your preferences and recommend movies or music based on patterns created by your past choices.

### Cloud computing

Cloud computing enables AI systems to use online networks for storing and processing large amounts of data globally. Cloud computing allows AI tools to perform complex tasks, such as natural language processing and data analysis, making advanced AI accessible on a global scale.
